1d:T2b4d,<p>If you don’t fill your life with your own priorities, the world will happily stuff its garbage in there instead.</p><p>By the end of this post, you’ll know exactly how to spot when your priorities have been hijacked ,  and, more importantly, how to get back on track and start living according to what actually matters to <em>you</em>.</p><p><em>If this post was forwarded to you, subscribe below</em></p><p><a href="https://www.worldsgreatestdoers.com/subscribe?" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Subscribe now</a></p><h2 id="part-1">Your Values Aren’t What You Say They Are ,  They’re What You’re Willing to Sacrifice For</h2><p>We all claim to value certain things: family, health, freedom, creativity, integrity. But talk is cheap. Real values are revealed by what we’re willing to give up, risk, or endure to protect them.</p><figure><a href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!6XAC!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9dc5c331-3ef1-45a1-9a8a-36b5e4ef0d74_1920x1080.jpeg" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank"><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!6XAC!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9dc5c331-3ef1-45a1-9a8a-36b5e4ef0d74_1920x1080.jpeg" alt="" loading="lazy" /></a></figure><h2 id="part-2">Introducing the Gumball Machine</h2><p>Imagine you are a gumball machine. The colorful gumballs inside represent your priorities ,  the things you care about most and choose to spend your limited time, energy, and attention on.</p><p>The core idea is simple:<br /><strong>If you don’t deliberately choose your own priorities, the world will choose them for you.</strong></p><p>There’s a strong gravitational pull at work here. When you leave your machine empty or undefended, society, family, social media, advertising, and peer pressure quickly insert their own agendas. Before long, you’re chasing goals that were never truly yours.</p><p>I first encountered this model in a <a href="https://youtu.be/3P9ySy8Z9ow?si=1zVPYmNpqYjcPvYH" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">fantastic video</a> by Mark Manson, the author of <a href="https://www.amazon.com/Subtle-Art-Not-Giving-Counterintuitive/dp/0062457713" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck</a>. While his video covers a lot of ground, we’re going to dive deeper into the Gumball Machine model,  how it exposes external values, and how to reclaim control.</p><h2 id="part-3">The Trap of External Values</h2><p>When you let the world fill your machine, you often don’t even realize the gumballs inside aren’t yours. You might spend years grinding away at a career, lifestyle, or image just to win approval from people whose opinions ultimately don’t matter.</p><p>A classic example: pursuing a prestigious career path not because it lights you up, but because parents, society, or status pushed it into your machine. Many people reach mid-life only to realize they’ve been living someone else’s dream.</p><p><a href="https://www.worldsgreatestdoers.com/p/the-gumball-machine?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Share</a></p><h2 id="part-4">The Litmus Test: Sacrifice + Courage</h2><p>So how do you know which gumballs are truly yours?</p><p>The Gumball Machine model offers a clear two-part test:</p><h3 id="part-5">1. Sacrifice</h3><p>Your true values aren’t defined by what you <em>say</em> you want ,  they’re defined by what you’re willing to <strong>lose</strong> or <strong>risk</strong> for them.</p><p>Take Rosa Parks in 1955. She refused to give up her seat on a segregated bus in Montgomery, Alabama, fully aware it could cost her freedom, safety, and livelihood. She lost her job as a seamstress and faced threats and harassment. Her willingness to endure arrest and ostracism proved that racial equality wasn’t just a nice idea to her ,  it was a genuine value worth sacrificing for.</p><p>If you’re not willing to lose <em>something</em> for a priority, it probably isn’t a real priority.</p><h3 id="part-6">2. Courage</h3><p>The second requirement is courage. To keep your own gumballs inside the machine, you must develop the willingness to be disliked.</p><p>Many people “go with the flow” to avoid conflict or disapproval. But living that way means handing the keys to your machine over to others. As the excellent book <em><a href="https://www.amazon.com/The-Courage-to-Be-Disliked-audiobook/dp/B07BRPW98K/ref=sr_1_1?adgrpid=187878930673&amp;dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.CAOvSGfFpvvBnBNAA6a70zvRlwpYg81ZGp18uevVKx6R3UgbjrmQyZlrfiUUGRhDODkkjeUCFUMSMs4bbeZXbnjs8e0URAv4ErQUls0py963lQ50w8TBgO4fgtEIWk-qKsICdacTJD5fcVWQcpgDonjqAkttYKHT6fuHuCs3-yRZOinFrLmhNAa-8pztaO55ohN7ez7eWv7Qgc05-kI9T0klol7kL0dLRqF2hEie9wQ.Hk9XMCKGbzmQ_pPmThnc6NBM8re8RqiCqTqf-fojGbw&amp;dib_tag=se&amp;hvadid=792923872497&amp;hvdev=c&amp;hvexpln=0&amp;hvlocphy=9060354&amp;hvnetw=g&amp;hvocijid=17596925568113497041--&amp;hvqmt=e&amp;hvrand=17596925568113497041&amp;hvtargid=kwd-329412961893&amp;hydadcr=15889_13882428_2448472&amp;keywords=the+courage+to+be+disliked&amp;mcid=871e52c89a9936059deee9a2974f4438&amp;qid=1774613395&amp;sr=8-1" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">The Courage to Be Disliked</a></em> (by Ichiro Kishimi and Fumitake Koga) teaches, it’s far better to be disliked for who you truly are than liked for a fake version of yourself that serves someone else’s values.</p><h2 id="part-7">Key Insight from <em>The Courage to Be Disliked</em>: Separation of Tasks</h2><p>One of the most practical tools in the book comes from Adlerian psychology: <strong>Separation of Tasks</strong>.</p><p>The idea is straightforward:<br />You are responsible for your own actions and feelings ,  <strong>not</strong> for how other people react to them.</p><p>The desire for recognition and approval is the root of the external-values trap. When you live for praise, you’re essentially adopting someone else’s value system.</p><p>The book presents this through a dialogue between a wise philosopher and a frustrated young man (the youth) who feels trapped by his parents’ expectations. The youth chose to become a librarian instead of joining the family business, and his parents disapproved.</p><p>The philosopher explains:<br />Your choice of career is <em>your</em> task.<br />How your parents feel about it (their sadness, anger, or disappointment) is <em>their</em> task ,  not yours.</p><p><a href="https://www.worldsgreatestdoers.com/subscribe?" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Subscribe now</a></p><p>The ultimate litmus test for any decision is this question:<br /><strong>“Who ultimately is going to receive the result brought about by the choice that is made?”</strong></p><p>If the emotional fallout belongs to someone else, it’s not your burden to carry. You can’t control their feelings, and you shouldn’t let them dictate your gumballs.</p>Text within this block will maintain its original spacing when published<em>“All you can do with regard to your own life is choose the best path that you believe in. On the other hand, what kind of judgment do other people pass on that choice? That is the task of other people, and is not a matter you can do anything about.” - The Philosopher</em><h2 id="part-8">The Price of Freedom</h2><p>True ownership of your priorities requires sacrificing the desire for universal approval. Being disliked by some people is often the proof that you’re actually exercising your freedom and living according to your own principles.</p><p>The philosopher in the book states it clearly:<br />“Freedom is being disliked by other people… The cost of freedom in interpersonal relationships is that one is disliked by other people.”</p><p>It’s uncomfortable. It can feel isolating. But constantly trying to please everyone is an extremely unfree ,  and ultimately impossible ,  way to live.</p><figure><a href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TjGU!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fac4a528e-f3e6-4fe7-a6b6-958f552675fe_1920x1080.jpeg" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank"><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TjGU!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fac4a528e-f3e6-4fe7-a6b6-958f552675fe_1920x1080.jpeg" alt="" loading="lazy" /></a></figure><h2 id="part-9">The Gordian Knot</h2><p>So how do you actually implement this and take back control of your gumball machine?</p><p>The philosopher uses the legend of the <strong>Gordian Knot</strong> as a perfect metaphor.</p><p>Legend says an impossibly intricate knot was tied by King Gordias, and whoever untied it would rule Asia. Many tried and failed. Then Alexander the Great arrived, drew his sword, and simply cut the knot in half.</p><p>Instead of painstakingly trying to “unravel” other people’s expectations through negotiation, explanation, or people-pleasing, you must take the sword of <strong>Separation of Tasks</strong> and cut the bond cleanly.</p><p><strong>Declare: “From here on, that is not my task.”</strong></p><p>It won’t always be easy. But as the philosopher reminds us: intervening in other people’s tasks and carrying their emotional burdens makes life heavy and exhausting. Learning to discard what isn’t yours is the first step toward a lighter, simpler, more authentic life.</p><p>If you aren’t willing to experience being disliked for what you believe in, then <strong>your gumballs are probably not your gumballs</strong>.</p><p>Take a moment right now to look inside your own mental gumball machine.<br />Which priorities are truly yours?</p><p>The world will always try to fill the empty spaces. Your job is to decide what belongs inside ,  and have the courage and willingness to sacrifice to keep it there.</p><p>Start today. Choose your own gumballs. Protect them fiercely. Live your life ,  not someone else’s.</p><p>- Dante</p><p><a href="https://www.worldsgreatestdoers.com/p/the-gumball-machine/comments" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Leave a comment</a></p><h4>My
