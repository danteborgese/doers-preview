1d:T19df,<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Push creative work from mediocre to excellent.</p><p><strong>Mental Model:</strong> <em>The Brain Trust</em> ,  A peer-based feedback system that removes hierarchy and ego to diagnose creative problems through institutionalized candor, without prescribing solutions or threatening the creator’s autonomy.</p><blockquote><p>Early on, all of our movies suck. Pixar films are not good at first, and our job is to make them so, to go, as I say, “from suck to not-suck.”</p></blockquote><p>,  Ed Catmull, <em><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Creativity-Inc-Expanded-Overcoming-Inspiration/dp/0593594649/ref=sr_1_1?adgrpid=193541636609&amp;dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.Ga33QekNnDJStz7w_lPvgxnMQKZBiM9ZUTWheW3fN5u2oPFq5YD_6vtHBYXtWwP3JahAI7AqU3gIT75Vyjdopld8km6BoodXuQACeqt5DBqJy-qRP58SI36nkMTfnfpsuQ5-l1RZNlAOjd39n1VRdxw10HE9w62Pl07hIMGfeCcQyy3Q88w-k9S3mtbB7-QR-ZdZqhn5nEv96WeZphoPWg3wn_m0Pc9U4LgXYGPpJ8o.JyfTEwNCOF3cHT_YWsyZrlyiUc7gCIQpE2qLxPMvN5I&amp;dib_tag=se&amp;hvadid=790121264533&amp;hvdev=c&amp;hvexpln=0&amp;hvlocphy=9060351&amp;hvnetw=g&amp;hvocijid=2932037664772325239--&amp;hvqmt=e&amp;hvrand=2932037664772325239&amp;hvtargid=kwd-76885650739&amp;hydadcr=22534_13730692_8442&amp;keywords=creativity+inc.&amp;mcid=4e2921fddbc338b7b9a4533d9b8ba400&amp;qid=1776691834&amp;sr=8-1" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Creativity, Inc.</a></em></p><p>That blunt truth powers Pixar’s greatest films. The tool that gets them there? The <strong>Braintrust</strong> ,  a peer-based feedback system that strips away hierarchy and ego to deliver brutally honest diagnoses of creative problems, while protecting the creator’s autonomy.</p><p><em>Was this email forwarded to you? Subscribe below</em></p><p><a href="https://www.worldsgreatestdoers.com/subscribe?" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Subscribe now</a></p><h3 id="part-1">What the Braintrust Actually Is</h3><p>It’s a collaborative peer-review meeting where experienced creatives review work-in-progress (a film sequence, product prototype, campaign, or design). The goal: push projects toward excellence and root out mediocrity through <strong>institutionalized candor</strong>.</p><p><strong>Candor</strong> is the quality of being open, honest, and straightforward ,  especially when delivering difficult or uncomfortable truths ,  without sugarcoating, hedging, or holding back.</p><h3 id="part-2">What It Is Not</h3><ul><li><p><strong>Not hierarchical.</strong> Job titles disappear. Even Steve Jobs was kept out because his presence would stifle honesty. Jeff Bezos does something similar: he speaks last so his view doesn’t color the room.</p></li><li><p><strong>Not prescriptive.</strong> The group diagnoses problems like doctors on a tough case ,  they don’t dictate the cure. Suggestions illustrate points, but the final call stays with the director/creator. Their solution is almost always better, but only after they hear from the braintrust.</p></li><li><p><strong>No decisions are made.</strong> Feedback is a gift, not an order. The creator decides what (or whether) to change. This removes defensiveness and keeps ownership intact.</p></li><li><p><strong>Not about egos.</strong> The project, not the person, goes under the microscope. You are not your idea.</p></li></ul><h3 id="part-3">Why It Works: Institutionalized Candor</h3><p>Creatives inevitably lose perspective ,  they fuse with the work and can’t see the forest for the trees. This is both normal and okay. The Braintrust provides fresh eyes without threatening autonomy.</p><p>A classic example from <em>The Incredibles</em>: The group said a Bob-and-Helen argument scene felt wrong ,  Bob came off as a bully. While the obvious solution was to change the words or tone used by Bob, the film’s director Brad Bird realized the real issue wasn’t the dialogue but the visuals: massive Bob dwarfed tiny Helen. He fixed it by having Helen stretch and hold her ground physically. No lines changed. The scene worked. The group identified the problem; the director found the elegant fix.</p><p>Pixar took this further with <strong>Notes Day</strong> ,  a full-company event where 1,200 employees generated thousands of ideas to fight creeping self-censorship. likely a byproduct of the company’s massive success. Chief Creative Officer John Lasseter opened by publicly sharing tough personal feedback on his own leadership, setting the vulnerability bar high.</p><p>Ultimately, Pixar’s model proves that "if there is more truth in the hallways than in meetings, you have a problem.”</p><p>Read <em><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Creativity-Inc-Expanded-Overcoming-Inspiration/dp/0593594649/ref=sr_1_1?adgrpid=193541636609&amp;dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.Ga33QekNnDJStz7w_lPvgxnMQKZBiM9ZUTWheW3fN5u2oPFq5YD_6vtHBYXtWwP3JahAI7AqU3gIT75Vyjdopld8km6BoodXuQACeqt5DBqJy-qRP58SI36nkMTfnfpsuQ5-l1RZNlAOjd39n1VRdxw10HE9w62Pl07hIMGfeCcQyy3Q88w-k9S3mtbB7-QR-ZdZqhn5nEv96WeZphoPWg3wn_m0Pc9U4LgXYGPpJ8o.JyfTEwNCOF3cHT_YWsyZrlyiUc7gCIQpE2qLxPMvN5I&amp;dib_tag=se&amp;hvadid=790121264533&amp;hvdev=c&amp;hvexpln=0&amp;hvlocphy=9060351&amp;hvnetw=g&amp;hvocijid=2932037664772325239--&amp;hvqmt=e&amp;hvrand=2932037664772325239&amp;hvtargid=kwd-76885650739&amp;hydadcr=22534_13730692_8442&amp;keywords=creativity+inc.&amp;mcid=4e2921fddbc338b7b9a4533d9b8ba400&amp;qid=1776691834&amp;sr=8-1" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Creativity, Inc</a>.</em> by Ed Catmull for the full story.
