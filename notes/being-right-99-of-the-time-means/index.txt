1b:T1378,<p>Humans are wired to see patterns. Our brains desperately connect dots and concoct stories, even when none exist. This is because 10,000 years ago, mistaking a rabbit in the bushes for a lion made you look silly, while the reverse got you eaten.</p><p>We’re still using that same ancient hardware today. </p><p><em>Was this post forwarded to you? Subscribe below.</em></p><p><a href="https://www.worldsgreatestdoers.com/subscribe?" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Subscribe now</a></p><h3 id="part-1">The Illusion of Patterns</h3><p>We’re “probability blind.” We turn random sequences into narratives and ignore independence.<br />Example: 1913 Monte Carlo roulette, black hit 26 times in a row. Gamblers lost millions betting on red because “it was due.” But each spin was independent of the previous one. The same is true for a coin toss. </p><h3 id="part-2">Survivorship Bias</h3><p>We only see winners. The losers stay invisible.</p><p>If you take an infinite number of monkeys and sit each one down in front of a typewriter, eventually one of them will type out <em>The Iliad</em>.</p><p>We’d then hire that monkey to write the sequel, forgetting it was pure chance, not talent. This happens all the time in real life. (with humans, not monkeys)</p><p>Better heuristic: <strong>Judge the process, not the outcome.</strong><br />$10M earned from playing Russian roulette is not the same as $10M earned from a career in dentistry. The people who died playing Russian roulette aren’t around to tell you that it’s a bad idea. But those who lived, aka The Survivors, will be happy to tell you how easy it was, and how anyone can earn $10M.</p><p>In 1,000 parallel lives, the Russian roulette guy dies ~167 times. The dentist doesn’t.</p><h3 id="part-3">Asymmetry &amp; Black Swans</h3><p>Being right 99% of the time is irrelevant if the 1% is fatal.</p><p><strong>Black Swans</strong> are rare, unpredictable events with massive impact. We only find out how to explain them <em>after</em> they happen.</p><p>Stop asking over “How likely will this happen?” and start asking <strong>“What are the consequences if it does?”</strong></p><p>You want the roof of your house to survive the once-in-a-century flood.</p><p>You want cheap equity in a start-up that, if successful, will be worth a lot of money. </p><p><a href="https://www.worldsgreatestdoers.com/p/being-right-99-of-the-time-means?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Share</a></p><p><strong>Bottom line:</strong> The world is disproportionately shaped by random, large-scale events, aka Black Swans. Master the mental model of randomness, and you will turn uncertainty into a competitive advantage: surviving the fatal 1% and capturing the life-changing upside.</p><p>- Dante</p><p><em>p.s.
