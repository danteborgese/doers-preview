19:T1858,<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Harness the power of compounding across knowledge, relationships, and decisions, not just money, to achieve extraordinary long-term results.</p><p><strong>Mental Model:</strong> <em>Compounding</em> ,  Small, consistent efforts multiply over time through exponential growth, creating disproportionate rewards that arrive at the end of the cycle.</p><p>Most people know compound interest, but they only apply it to their 401(k). This is a huge mistake.</p><p>Your brain is wired for linear thinking, so it chronically underestimates this force. Early progress feels invisible.</p><p>Thanks for reading Two Minute Wisdom! Subscribe for free to receive new posts and support my work.</p><p>The rewards are heavily <strong>back-loaded</strong>.</p><p>To win, you must reinvest every return, whether it’s money, knowledge, trust, or skills, rather than cashing out early.</p><h3 id="part-1">Play Iterated Games</h3><p>Life’s best opportunities aren’t one-shot deals. They’re repeated games with the same people over decades.</p><p>Silicon Valley operates as a high-trust, iterated game. The same founders and investors cycle through multiple companies over decades. You don’t just work with someone once ,  you work with them across three startups, two funding rounds, and a dozen introductions.</p><p>In a one-time transaction, the incentive is to maximize your own gain ,  even at the other person’s expense. But when you know you’ll see the same people again in six months, two years, or the next company, the only rational strategy is to make them rich too.</p><ul><li><p>Investors who screw founders get quietly blacklisted</p></li><li><p>Founders who treat employees poorly can’t recruit for their next venture</p></li></ul><p>Pick high-integrity people and environments, then stay. Switching industries every few years resets your compounding to zero. Your character and reputation eventually become your most valuable assets.</p><h3 id="part-2">Create Unpredictable Options</h3><p>Compounding knowledge turns you into an idea machine. Each new concept improves your ability to connect and understand old ones. This is what David Epstein writes about in his book <em><a href="https://davidepstein.com/range/" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">Range.</a></em></p><p>Invest in high-quality capabilities today. In complex systems, future opportunities are unknowable. Compounded advantages create the unpredictable options that separate the extraordinary from the average.</p><h3 id="part-3">Munger’s Rule of Non-Interruption</h3><p>Charlie Munger called compound interest the eighth wonder of the world. His deepest insight is that the hard part is simply never interrupting it.</p><figure><a href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Nly7!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff7e20d90-101f-449f-b144-74627d8e4991_1920x1080.jpeg" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank"><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Nly7!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff7e20d90-101f-449f-b144-74627d8e4991_1920x1080.jpeg" alt="" loading="lazy" /></a></figure><p>Munger practiced “sit-on-your-ass investing.” Buy great businesses and hold for decades without fiddling. The same applies to life. Build reputation, wisdom, and character through unbroken continuity.</p><p>A good reputation compounds over decades but can be destroyed in minutes. Reliability beats raw talent when multiplied by time.</p><p>Munger’s formula for success: Take a simple idea seriously, for a very long time. Slug it out one inch at a time. Be consistently not stupid rather than occasionally brilliant.</p><p>Apply compounding across knowledge, relationships, and decisions, and the game of life tilts dramatically in your favor.</p><p>- Dante</p><p><em>P.S.
